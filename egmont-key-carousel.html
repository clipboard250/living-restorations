What Anchors the Dunes
Sea Oats
Uniola paniculata

The dominant native on Egmont. Deep roots hold the dunes together through storms, and the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service has long used prescribed burns every 5–10 years to keep sea oat habitat healthy.

What Survives Fire
Saw Palmetto
Serenoa repens

One of the most fire-adapted plants in Florida. The growing tip sits at or below ground, so saw palmetto can resprout within weeks of a burn. Individual plants can live over 500 years.

What Stands Through It
Cabbage Palm
Sabal palmetto

Florida's state tree. The thick fibrous trunk insulates the living tissue inside, which is why cabbage palms can often survive ground fires while the surrounding scrub burns and regrows around them.
