The Basics

What is a living shoreline?

A living shoreline is a way to protect the edge of your property using nature's own materials. Native grasses, oyster shell, and natural fiber slow incoming waves, hold sand and soil in place, and give fish, birds, and crabs a place to live.

A seawall stops water at a hard line. A living shoreline takes the energy out of the water before it gets there, and it can keep building on itself as plants spread and oysters settle.

  • 🌾
    Native marsh plantsFlorida-native grasses with deep roots that trap sediment and soften wave action.
  • 🦪
    Oyster habitatShell and reef structure that breaks waves offshore and filters the water as oysters grow.
  • 🪵
    Natural, biodegradable materialsFiber logs and similar materials that hold everything in place while plants take root.

Your Options

Living shoreline or seawall?

Not every site suits the same answer. Water depth, wave exposure, and what is already there all matter. Kiamesha reads the site first, then recommends what fits.

🧱

Seawall only

A hard vertical wall holds the line, but waves bounce off it instead of slowing down. It offers little habitat and needs repair or replacement over time.

🌊

Living shoreline

Native plants and oyster habitat absorb wave energy, hold sediment, and create habitat. It can grow and adjust as conditions change.

🤝

Hybrid: plants in front of your wall

Already have a seawall? Native plants or oyster habitat placed in front of it can soften waves and add habitat without removing the wall.

What's New in 2026

Florida is making living shorelines easier.

New state support means this is a good time for waterfront owners to look at a living shoreline.

📋

A faster permit process

A Florida law signed in 2026 directs the Department of Environmental Protection to create a faster permitting process for living shorelines. FDEP is still setting the details, and we keep up with them for you.

Grant eligibility

The same law makes living shoreline projects eligible for Resilient Florida grant funding. Ask us whether your project may qualify.
